Accountability within a University - A Case Study for Staff Development

Under construction

Concocted from a Number of Different Real Events

Personae Dramatis

  • University
  • Professional Institute
  • Head of Department
  • Course Director
  • Module Leader (Of the Professional Practice module.)

Scenario

  • A postgraduate course offered by the University promises a route to membership of a professional institute.
  • There is a deadline for submission – each student’s application must be received within 12 months of them completing the course.
  • One of the module leaders is tasked with submitting applications to the institute on behalf of students who have successfully completed the course.
  • This same module leader has completed the applications every year for the past eight years.
  • The module leader has experienced a serious health issue over the last year and has needed numerous periods of sick leave to recover from treatments.
  • The module leader is now on long term sick leave and may never return to work.

Scene 1

  • A student who completed the course gets in touch with the course director to ask.
    “What happened to our membership of the institute?
    I’ve talk to some of the other students and none of us has heard from the institute.
    Shouldn’t we have received certificates by now.”
  • The course director hasn’t been involved directly in the application process before and is a bit unsure on how to follow up. Ey search through teamwork systems, shared disk drives, committee minutes and everywhere else looking for information about the process.
  • There doesn’t seem to be any information relating to the process but a close colleague of the module leader says: “I think ey probably kept all the information on the hard disk of eir laptop.”
  • The laptop was wiped and returned to IT some months ago.
  • An application is put into IT to gain access to module leader’s email account but there is precedent and it doesn’t look hopeful.
  • The course director contacts the institute. The contact there says: “We always deal directly with module leader on this process by email. Normally the applications come through around September each year but we’ve had nothing this year. I can see records of several emails to module leader about this and the last one bounced back with an out of office message. You’ve only got three weeks left to meet our deadline.”

    • Question – thoughts so far?

    Scene 2

    Crisis meetings in the University.

    • Ultimate legal accountability to the students? The University, not any employee, not even the vice chancellor is accountable before the law because the University has legal personality and it was the University that made promises about the course, that took the fees and delivered the course. If there is legal action it will be against the University.
    • Within the University, who is accountable to whom? At what point is that question relevant?
    • Can the crisis be resolved within the next few weeks?

    Further discovery of information:

    • It turns out that the module leader had raised concerns with the course director over the last year about the work load associated with processing applications to the Institute and in consideration of eir health status had requested that they train up a colleague with a view to taking over the task. No conclusions were reached.
    • It also turns out that the course director doesn’t know anything about the process of liaising with the Institute – working that out was left to the module leader.
    • The module leader was never asked to document the process in any shared information store and worked from experience over several years and by referring to old email trails.
    • The head of department had never asked the course director about how the process was run or even who ran it.

    Question – thoughts so far?

    Scene 3

    • The crisis is resolved by a number of staff in the department working together.
    • An administrator from the institute goes above and beyond to explain the application process to the course director and some other academic staff.
    • Teams who run student information systems at the University drop other tasks and work flat out to obtain data required.
    • Applications are not ready quite in time for the institute’s deadline but an extension is offered and that new deadline is met.
    • Two months later students receive certificates in the post.

    Scene 4

    Postmortem

    • After a certain amount of mudslinging and various permutations of “you are accountable” the head of department intervenes: “Some of us are just using the word accountable as a euphemism for blame. Let’s stop talking about blame for how this went and start using the word accountable in its proper sense. How are we going to improve the way we work and communicate with each other?”
    • Some decisions are made:

      • Accountability for processing membership applications to the institute for graduates will be assigned to a number of people in various roles:
      • Head of Department – will commit to looking at the progress of the process on key dates in the calendar and will examine and approve the documented workflow.
      • Course Director – will devise and document the workflow and assign colleagues to implement it. Will periodically review progress and will respond to requests for guidance from those who implement it. Will provide shared read-only access to the workflow documentation to relevant services in the university so they can better offer support to the department. Will review implementation and intervene quickly if colleagues are not recording information in the right places or are improvising off of the agreed workflow. Will respond promptly with decisions to suggestions about changing the workflow – for example if the Institute changes its processes.
      • Others – academic and administrative staff in the department will implement the workflow, record progress in the agreed locations and notify the course director of the completion or failure of key events. Will provide feedback on the workflow in the light of experience.
      • Accountability will not just be shared horizontally between team members on the same grade but vertically up the management chain. The nature of the accountability of each person across the team and up the management chain will be defined, explicit and two-way.
      • The team will stop using email to each other’s personal addresses as a means of communication on this task – a proper, shared task progress store will be used so that everyone all the way up to the head of department can monitor progress.
      • Lessons learned will be applied to numerous other processes in the department moving away from individual junior staff carrying the can for carrying out tasks that are complex, time consuming and mission critical.

    Questions – Reflection on the Scenario

    For this particular job within the department, in the future…

    • If the course director is accountable to the head of department, is the head of department also accountable to the course director?
    • How?
    • Given the decisions that were made, is accountability still just a euphemism for blame?
    • If so, why do you think that?
    • If not, in what way is it different from blame?
    • Why did the staff running student information systems put in special effort to help the department meet the deadline at the expense of their other tasks? Was it because they were accountable for applications of students to the institute? If not, why else?

    More Questions - Anxiety and Resistance

    Is it possible that individuals might work against accountability even in an environment of excellent guidance on how to work together and with readily available tools to support workflow design and record keeping? Questions for discussion:

    • Could some junior members of staff find it intimidating to be asked to make reports of progress that are visible to a number of other people?
    • What might they do under pressure to avoid scrutiny?
    • How would the style and personality of their line manager affect that?
    • What if eir line manager made it clear that since ey designed the workflow, ey would be sharing accountability for any difficulties in implementing it?
    • Could some line managers feel intimidated if they are asked to take an active role in designing the workflows that junior staff will carry out?
    • Could some line managers feel that they are less likely to attract blame if they simply tell junior staff to achieve some outcomes and stay blissfully ignorant of how they do it?
    • How would that be affected by the attitude of their line manager?
    • What if the line manager’s line manager pointed out that ey too will share responsibility by reviewing the work flow design and approving it?
    • What about initiative? What about individuals taking action without a lot of discussion?
    • Accountability is about effective design of workflows, record keeping and good communication up and down the management line and side to side within teams of peers.
    • Any task must have at least two defined and properly recorded roles within it – not just for the person(s) who implement(s) it but also for those who need to monitor, guide and report on progress.
    • What about initiative? What about small things that can’t be part of a workflow? What if the consequences of failure are slight, the benefits of success great but the line manager is on annual leave? If a talented individual has a proven track record of good judgement, safe decision making and a willingness to highlight failure instead of covering it up… line managers might encourage that initiative but temper it by highlighting the need to record what actions were taken, report back at the next opportunity and modify future action based on feedback.
    • However, even that can be covered by a single, team-wide, defined workflow with a defined record keeping protocol just like any other workflow.
