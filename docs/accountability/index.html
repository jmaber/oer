Accountability within a University - A Case Study for Staff Development

by Jon Maber

See bottom of page for licensing. See appendix for suggestions about use of this case study.

Concocted from a Number of Different Real Events, Fictionalised and Exaggerated

TL;DR? Conventional wisdom says that excessively text-heavy educational materials are to be discouraged. I make no apologies - this is aimed at University employees who should be educated to a level that allows them to digest text! Also, although this text is provided in one page that is for the benefit of other authors and trainers - it is expected that the text would be split into pieces for presentation to workshop participants and adorned with graphics and local material. More on this in the appendix.

Dramatis Personae

Organisations

  • A University
  • A National Professional Institute

Principals

  • Head of Department
  • Course Director - Joined the University a year ago
  • One of the Module Leaders - Member of department going back decades - On extended sick leave
  • University Legal Advisor

Supporting

  • Other Staff in the Department both Academic and Administrative
  • University Student Information System Team
  • Administrators in the Professional Institute

Scenario

  • A postgraduate course offered by the University promises a route to membership of a professional institute.
  • There is a deadline for submission – each student’s application must be received within 12 months of them completing the course.
  • One of the module leaders is tasked with submitting applications to the institute on behalf of students who have successfully completed the course.
  • This same module leader has completed the applications every year for the past eight years.
  • The module leader has experienced a serious health issue over the last year and has needed numerous periods of sick leave to recover from treatments.
  • The module leader is now on long term sick leave and may never return to work.

Scene 1

  • A student who completed the course gets in touch with the course director to ask.
    What happened to our membership of the institute?
    I’ve talk to some of the other students and none of us has heard from the institute.
    Shouldn’t we have received certificates by now?
  • The course director only joined the University recently, wasn't aware of this task and is a bit unsure on how to follow up. Ey searches through teamwork systems, shared disk drives, committee minutes and everywhere else looking for information about the process.
  • There doesn’t seem to be any information relating to the process but a close colleague of the module leader says:
    I think ey probably kept all the information on the hard disk of eir laptop.
  • The laptop was wiped and returned to IT some months ago.
  • An application is put into IT to gain access to module leader’s email account but there is precedent for denying access on grounds of privacy and it doesn’t look hopeful.
  • The course director contacts the institute. The contact there says:
    We always deal directly with module leader on this process by email.
    Normally the applications come through around September each year but we’ve had nothing this year.
    I can see records of several emails to module leader about this and the last one bounced back with an out of office message.
    We didn't have any other contact details.
    You’ve only got three weeks left to meet our deadline.

Question – thoughts so far?

Scene 2

Crisis meetings in the University.

  • Ultimately where does accountability lie if graduates are disqualified from membership of the Institute? The University legal expert explains;
    The University, not any department, not any employee, not even the vice chancellor is accountable before the law because the University has legal personality and it was the University that made promises about the course, that took the fees and delivered the course.
    If there is legal action by graduates it will be against 'The University'.
  • Within the University, who is accountable to whom? The legal expert advises:
    This is not a question for me because accountability within the law is not relevant.
    This is a different kind of accountability. I suggest you leave this question until after you have made a concerted effort to meet the deadline.
  • Can the crisis be resolved within the next few weeks?

Further discovery of information:

  • It turns out that the module leader had raised concerns with the course director over the last year about the work load associated with processing applications to the Institute and in consideration of eir health status had requested that they train up a colleague with a view to taking over the task. No conclusions were reached.
  • It also turns out that the course director doesn’t know anything about the process of liaising with the Institute. That was worked out years ago by the previous course director and then delegated to the module leader.
  • The module leader was never asked to document the process in any shared information store and worked from experience over several years and by referring to old email trails.
  • The head of department does not remember asking the past or present course director about how the process works. And didn't know that it had been delegated.
  • The head of department gets a grip of things
    Let's leave the post mortem for later and all pull together to meet the deadline.
    I and the course director will meet today and put together a plan of action.

Question – thoughts so far?

Scene 3

  • The crisis is resolved by a number of staff in the department working together. They volunteer to help without any pressure being applied.
  • An administrator from the institute goes above and beyond to explain the application process to the course director and some other academic and administrative staff in the department.
  • Teams who run student information systems at the University drop other tasks and work flat out to obtain data required. No pressure was put on them to help in this way.
  • Even the head of department pitches in and takes a share of student applications to prepare.
  • Applications are not ready quite in time for the institute’s deadline.
  • The Vice Chancellor of the University has a direct conversation with the Chair of the Board of the Institute and an extension is offered.
  • The applications all go in before the extended deadline.
  • Two months later students receive certificates in the post.

Questions

  • Why did so many staff in the department pitch in and help even though they have never previously been involved in the task and are not accountable for it?
  • Why did the staff running student information systems put in special effort to help the department meet the deadline at the expense of their other tasks?
  • If the same crisis occurs next year in the same department do you they will want to pitch in and help again?

Scene 4

Postmortem

  • After a certain amount of mudslinging and various permutations of “you are accountable” the head of department intervenes:
    Some of us are just using the word accountable as a euphemism for blame.
    Let’s stop talking about blame for how this went wrong and start using the word accountable in its proper sense.
    How are we going to improve the way we do this in the future?
  • Some decisions are made:
    Accountability for processing membership applications to the institute for graduates will be assigned to a number of people in various roles:
    • Head of Department –
      Will commit to looking at the progress of the process on key dates in the calendar and will examine and approve the documented workflow.
    • Course Director –
      Will devise and document the workflow and assign colleagues to implement it.
      Will periodically review progress and will respond to requests for guidance from those who implement it.
      Will provide shared read-only access to the workflow documentation to relevant services in the university so they can better offer support to the department.
      Will review implementation and intervene quickly if colleagues are not recording information in the right places or are improvising off of the agreed workflow.
      Will respond promptly with decisions to suggestions about changing the workflow – for example if the Institute changes its processes.
      Will ensure that the head of department has access to progress records.
    • Others –
      Academic and administrative staff in the department will implement the workflow, record progress in the agreed locations and alert the course director of the completion or failure of key events. Will provide feedback on the workflow in the light of experience.
    • Accountability will not just be shared horizontally between team members on the same grade but vertically up and down the management chain. The nature of the accountability of each person across the team and both up and back down the management chain will be defined, explicit, two-way and recorded.
    • The team will stop using email to each other’s personal addresses as a means of communication on this task – a more appropriate IT tool will be used so that the process will continue smoothly if individuals become unavailable at critical points.
    • A formal change management system will be used to handle year by year changes to processes. Everyone involved in the task will have access to it. Anyone can record a change request but only staff at defined levels of management can approve change requests.
    • Lessons learned will be applied to numerous other processes in the department moving away from individual junior staff carrying the can for carrying out tasks that are complex, time consuming and mission critical.
    • With each process that is formalised, staff will be more familiar with the tools and methods and the management of change will get easier and smoother.

Questions – Reflection on the Scenario

For this particular job within the department, in the future…

  • What do you think the head of department meant by the word accountable in its proper sense?
  • If the course director is accountable to the head of department, is the head of department also accountable to the course director?
  • How?
  • Given the decisions that were made, is accountability still just a euphemism for blame?
  • If so, why do you think that?
  • If not, in what way is it different from blame?

More Questions - Anxiety and Resistance

Is it possible that individuals might work against accountability even in an environment of excellent guidance on how to work together and with readily available tools to support workflow design and record keeping? Questions for discussion:

  • Could some junior members of staff find it intimidating to be asked to make reports of progress that are visible to a number of other people?
  • What might they do to avoid scrutiny if they feel that way?
  • How would the style and personality of their line manager affect that?
  • What if eir line manager made it clear that since ey designed the workflow, ey would be sharing accountability for any difficulties in implementing it?
  • Could some line managers feel intimidated if they are asked to take an active role in designing the workflows that junior staff will carry out?
  • Could some line managers feel that they are less likely to attract blame if they simply tell junior staff to achieve some outcomes and remain blissfully ignorant of how they do it?
  • How would that be affected by the attitude of their line manager?
  • What if the line manager’s line manager pointed out that ey too will share responsibility by reviewing the work flow design and approving it?
  • What about initiative? What about individuals taking action without a lot of discussion?

Take Home Messages

This is the section that will most need adaptation for your organisation. However, if your take home messages are radically different to mine you might have chosen the wrong case study for your workshop! Remember to give your participants a link to this page so they can see which conclusions are yours and which mine.
  • Accountability is about effective design of workflows, record keeping and good communication up and down the management line and side to side within teams of peers.
  • Any task must have at least two defined and properly recorded roles within it – not just for the person(s) who implement(s) it but also for those who need to monitor, guide and report on progress.
  • Ultimately, legal accountability is collective - the corporate 'person' is accountable to its students. When we talk about accountability of individuals within the organisation this is always in relation to each other.
  • In a University it is often a genuine and heartfelt desire to give students a good education that motivates people in a University to accept their portion of accountability. However, if that isn't a motivation for some, then seeing others, especially more senior colleagues acting in a ways that support collective accountability is a good second best motivation.
  • What about initiative? What about small things that can’t be part of a workflow? What if the consequences of failure are slight, the benefits of success great but the line manager is on annual leave?

    If a talented individual has a proven track record of good judgement, safe decision making and a willingness to highlight failure instead of covering it up…

    ...line managers might encourage that initiative but temper it by highlighting the need to record what actions were taken, report back at the next opportunity and modify future action based on feedback.

    However, even that can be covered by a single, team-wide, defined workflow with a defined record keeping protocol just like any other workflow!

Appendix

What for?

This case study is intended for use in a staff development workshop in a University. It assumes an audience of highly educated participants capable of forming their own opinions and with low tolerance for being patronised. I've tried not to make too many conclusions myself and I suggest that you allow participants to reach their own conclusions too. I hope this approach will allow the case study to fit in with most work cultures.

This document is deliberately unadorned with graphics, colours and other fancy styling. I leave that for you to add as appropriate to your organisation. I have used very plain and standards compliant HTML tags - when you create a modified version please give equal consideration to standard compliance and accessibility.

I wouldn't present this material to workshop participants as a single block of text as it appears on this GitHub site. It needs to be broken up into pieces and released bit by bit. You might even consider using it as a script and make a performance of it instead of putting text on a screen. However, if you do that please give consideration the needs of participants. For example you may want to give the script to certain participants in advance so they can brief their personal assistants, sign language interpreters etc.

It might take just 10 minutes to read the text in its entirety but your workshop might consist of components like these:

  • Participants discussing the issues
  • Application of conclusions to the local organisation
  • Compare aspects of the case study with the actual experiences of participants
  • Drawing together consensus among participants about what accountability means
  • Asking participants if they have a different attitude or conception of accountability now
  • Will participants promote ideas developed in the workshop among colleagues?
  • Do participants feel they are empowered to promote these ideas?
  • Opportunities for more senior participants to comment on how/whether they will take this further.

The case study might provide a backbone to a workshop lasting several hours. My contribution will be trivial compared to the workshop facilitators and participants!

Why?

I was motivated to create this by seeing some staff development materials on accountability that I found subtly negative and divisive. They contained amateur psychology without citation of sources. When I traced the origins of the Psychology used I found it came from academic literature in the field of family therapy. It was part of a technique to use with families that include a member with a psychiatric condition. The message seemed to be that junior staff who act in an unaccountable way are suffering from some kind of pathology and need to be gently but firmly made to accept that they are defective in some way. There was no mention of how accountability might be shared between junior and senior staff.

I wanted to enable an approach to the subject that accepts that pretty much everyone in a university wants to make a positive contribution and that no one is psychologically flawed. I also wanted to highlight that developing personal accountability is much less important than development of a network of two-way relationships of accountability across the entire organisation.

I might add that in the unlikely event that a particular member of staff does have some pathological attitude towards accountability, the worst way to approach this challenge would be to put them in a room full of people and workshop the issue. Would you allow all participants to infer who in the room the message is aimed at? Hope the targeted person gets the message? A private, confidential and candid discussion between line manager and colleague would surely be more appropriate.
